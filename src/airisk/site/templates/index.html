How fast are the drivers of AI risk moving?

Nobody can measure the probability that AI destroys humanity. What can be measured is how quickly the things that would drive such a catastrophe are changing: how powerful AI systems are, how long they work without supervision, how good they are at hacking, how much paid work they can do, how much money is racing in, and how many harms are already being reported. This board tracks {{ board|length }} of those indicators from open data and asks of each one what you would ask of a clock: is it moving faster or slower than it was?

Outages and hacks blamed on AI

Curated from news reports{% if events.searched %} and searched again each week (last {{ events.searched }}){% else %}, as of {{ events.as_of }}{% endif %}. Each row keeps apart what happened, the cause the organisation states, and who links it to AI.
{% set w = events.wave %}

Between 12 and 17 November 2026, {{ w|length }} large outages hit in a row: {% for e in w|reverse %}{{ e.org }}{% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %}. None has an official AI cause. Each organisation blames its own bug, update, maintenance or router, and officials say none was a cyberattack and that they are not connected. The AI explanations range from speculation, such as the Homeland Security Secretary passing on a theory that "it's the SI" (superintelligence), to an anonymously sourced claim that rushed patches for flaws found by AI overloaded systems. The Director of National Intelligence tied the NSA outage to patching for a PHP vulnerability. AI-assisted research had found PHP flaws that autumn.

The clearest AI harms of the year are elsewhere: in {{ events.counts['ai-acted'] }} cases an AI system acted on its own and its maker said so, and in {{ events.counts['ai-used'] }} investigators, the FBI among them, say attackers used AI tools to do the work. These are events, not a rate, so they are not on the pace board. They are what the hacking benchmark measures in the lab, showing up in the world.

How to read this. Every number is recomputed from the sources on each refresh. "Latest best" is the best model measured so far; "pace" is a straight-line fit (on a log scale for things that grow by multiplying) over the last window, compared with the window before. A board full of red does not mean doom is near, and a green one does not mean safety: it says how fast the inputs to the risk are moving. Safety research, regulation and safeguards are not on the board because no open dataset tracks them over time — the paper says what is missing.
